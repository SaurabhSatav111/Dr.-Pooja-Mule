Stretch Marks During Pregnancy: Everything You Should Know

PM
Dr. Pooja Mote (Mule) • June 05, 2026 • 5 min read

What Are Stretch Marks?

Almost 8 out of 10 women develop stretch marks during pregnancy. As a Gynecologist in Sambhajinagar, I know most of you are concerned — but they don't produce any risk to you or your baby, nor are they painful.

Why Do They Happen?

They occur because your body grows faster than your skin can keep up with. Hormonal changes during pregnancy also relax the skin's collagen fibres, making them more likely to tear. When the skin stretches rapidly, the elastic fibres beneath the surface tear slightly, leaving marks.

Who Is at Higher Risk?

  • Lighter-skinned individuals
  • Younger women
  • Genetic predisposition (family history of stretch marks)
  • Rapid weight gain during pregnancy
  • Multiple pregnancies (twins or triplets)
  • Corticosteroid use

Where Do They Appear?

Mainly on the tummy, upper thighs, breasts, and hips. Depending on your skin color, they can start off looking red, pink, purple, or brown. Over time, and especially after delivery, they will fade into a much less noticeable silvery grey color.

Can They Be Prevented?

While you cannot completely prevent them if you are genetically predisposed, keeping your skin hydrated, using moisturizers like cocoa butter creams, drinking plenty of water, and managing your weight gain can help. Always consult your gynecologist before using any topical treatments during pregnancy to ensure they are safe for your baby.
