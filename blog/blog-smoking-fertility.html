How Smoking Affects Your Fertility & Chances of Pregnancy

PM
Dr. Pooja Mote (Mule) • June 10, 2026 • 4 min read

Smoking and Fertility — What the Research Shows

Somebody who smokes takes more time to get pregnant than non-smokers. Second-hand smoke is almost as harmful — ladies exposed to second-hand smoke also take longer to conceive.

How Does Tobacco Harm Reproductive Health?

The synthetic compounds in tobacco can directly harm eggs and sperm, affecting a future child's health. Nicotine and carbon monoxide reduce blood flow to the reproductive organs and disrupt hormonal balance.

Effects on Women

  • Disrupts ovulation cycle
  • Damages egg quality
  • Reduces ovarian reserve
  • Increases risk of miscarriage
  • Affects hormone levels (FSH, LH, estrogen)

Effects on Men

  • Reduces sperm count
  • Lowers sperm motility
  • Increases abnormal sperm
  • Damages sperm DNA
  • Reduces testosterone levels

What Should You Do?

The best step is to quit smoking before trying to conceive. Both partners should stop at least 3 months before attempting pregnancy. Consult Dr. Pooja for a pre-conception plan tailored to you.
